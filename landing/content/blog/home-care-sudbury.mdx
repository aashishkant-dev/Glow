Greater Sudbury is home to over 160,000 people, a significant portion of whom are seniors or adults requiring some form of in-home support. Yet for many families, navigating the home care landscape here feels needlessly complex.

This guide cuts through the confusion. Here's what you need to know about accessing quality home care in Sudbury — from publicly funded services to private-pay options.

## The publicly funded system: CCAC and Home Care Ontario

Ontario's publicly funded home care is managed through Home and Community Care Support Services (HCCSS), previously known as the Community Care Access Centres (CCAC). If you or a family member qualifies, this system can provide subsidized PSW services, nursing visits, and therapy.

The challenge: waitlists. For non-urgent cases in Northern Ontario, wait times for publicly funded PSW services can extend weeks or months. For families dealing with a sudden health change — a stroke, a fall, a hospitalization discharge — that timeline is simply not workable.

## Private-pay care: filling the gap

Private-pay home care has grown significantly in Northern Ontario precisely because of this gap. Families who need care now, on their schedule, turn to private arrangements rather than waiting.

Private-pay PSWs in Sudbury typically charge between $22 and $35 per hour, depending on specialization, experience, and whether they're placed through an agency or a platform. CareNearby standardizes this at $25/hr with full transparency.

## What to expect from a PSW visit in Sudbury

Most PSW visits in Greater Sudbury follow a similar structure. A visit typically runs 2-4 hours and includes some combination of:

- Personal care assistance (bathing, grooming, oral hygiene)
- Meal preparation or feeding support
- Light housekeeping directly related to the client's wellbeing
- Companionship and social engagement
- Mobility support and fall prevention

PSWs are trained to work within clearly defined scope-of-practice guidelines. They do not administer medications, perform medical procedures, or provide nursing care. These distinctions matter — particularly for families managing complex medical conditions.

## Finding PSWs in Sudbury

There are three main ways families in Sudbury find private-pay PSW support:

**1. Private agencies** — Agencies pre-screen and place caregivers, but typically charge 30–40% markup on the caregiver's rate. Some offer consistency guarantees; others do not.

**2. Word of mouth / direct hire** — Common in smaller communities like Sudbury. Lower cost, but carries risks around verification, tax obligations, and insurance.

**3. Platforms like CareNearby** — Purpose-built marketplaces that verify credentials, handle payments, and provide accountability through ratings — without the agency markup.

## Northern Ontario considerations

Sudbury has characteristics that shape how home care works here:

- **Geography**: Greater Sudbury is Canada's largest municipality by area. A caregiver in New Sudbury and one in Chelmsford may be 30 minutes apart — proximity matters for consistent care.
- **Bilingualism**: A significant portion of the Sudbury population is francophone. Ensuring your caregiver can communicate comfortably in your family's preferred language is worth prioritizing.
- **Weather**: Winter conditions in Sudbury are real. Build flexibility into your care schedule for snowstorms and road conditions.

## When to consider a transition to residential care

Home care — even intensive home care — isn't always the right long-term answer. Signs that it may be time to consider other options include:

- Safety concerns that home care cannot adequately address
- Caregiver burnout in family members providing supplemental care
- Escalating medical complexity requiring 24/7 professional supervision
- The client's own expressed preference for a different setting

This is a deeply personal decision. Many Sudbury families benefit from speaking with a social worker at Health Sciences North or a geriatric care manager before making changes.
