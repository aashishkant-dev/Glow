A dementia diagnosis changes everything for a family. Routines that were once simple become unpredictable. Safety concerns emerge. And the person you love may need help they can't always ask for.

For many families in Greater Sudbury, keeping a loved one at home — rather than transitioning to a long-term care facility — is both a practical goal and a deeply personal one. This guide explains how in-home PSW support makes that possible.

## Signs your loved one may need in-home help

Dementia progresses gradually, and families often adapt so incrementally that the need for support sneaks up on them. Watch for:

- **Forgetting meals or medication** — skipping meals, not taking prescriptions, or taking them twice
- **Personal hygiene declining** — resistance to bathing, wearing the same clothes for days
- **Wandering or getting lost** — confusion about time of day, leaving the house at night
- **Safety hazards at home** — leaving stoves on, unlocked doors, falls
- **Isolation and mood changes** — withdrawal from conversation, increased anxiety, agitation in late afternoon (sundowning)
- **Caregiver fatigue** — if a family member is providing daily care and showing signs of exhaustion or resentment

Any of these signals is a reasonable trigger to explore additional support.

## What a PSW can do for a person with dementia

Personal Support Workers who work with dementia clients provide structured, compassionate daily assistance. Their role typically includes:

- **Morning routines** — bathing, dressing, grooming in a calm, predictable sequence
- **Meal preparation and feeding support** — preparing safe, nutritious meals and encouraging eating
- **Medication reminders** — ensuring prescriptions are taken on schedule (reminders only, not administration)
- **Companionship and engagement** — conversation, light activities, music, and routine that reduces agitation
- **Mobility and safety monitoring** — preventing falls, accompanying on walks, supervising movement in the home
- **Respite for family caregivers** — giving primary caregivers a scheduled break

## Safety tips for dementia care at home in Northern Ontario

Sudbury's winters create specific challenges for dementia care at home:

- **Lock or alarm exterior doors** — wandering outdoors in winter carries serious risks
- **Simplify the home environment** — reduce clutter and clearly label cabinets and rooms
- **Use nightlights throughout** — confusion worsens in the dark; keep paths lit
- **Maintain routine even in winter** — consistent wake, meal, and bedtime rhythms reduce agitation
- **Keep emergency contacts visible** — by the phone and on the fridge in large print

## Respite care for family caregivers

If you're the primary caregiver for a family member with dementia, respite is not a luxury — it's a safety requirement. Caregiver burnout leads to poor outcomes for both the caregiver and the person being cared for.

A PSW visiting three to five times per week can provide the relief family caregivers need to stay healthy, work, and show up fully when they're present.
