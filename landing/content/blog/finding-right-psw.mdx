Choosing a personal support worker is one of the most significant decisions a family can make. You're inviting someone into your home, trusting them with a loved one's care, and placing enormous faith in a process that — without the right tools — can feel overwhelming.

This guide walks you through the key steps to finding a PSW who is not just qualified, but genuinely right for your family.

## 1. Understand what you actually need

Before you search, get specific about the type of care your family member requires. PSWs offer a wide range of services:

- Personal hygiene assistance (bathing, grooming, dressing)
- Mobility support and transfer assistance
- Medication reminders (not administration — that's a different role)
- Meal preparation and feeding
- Companionship and emotional support
- Light housekeeping

Write down which of these apply to your situation, and be honest about frequency. A few hours twice a week is a very different arrangement than full-day coverage.

## 2. Prioritize verified credentials

In Ontario, a PSW must complete an approved program at a college or private career college. This is not optional — it's the baseline. When reviewing candidates, always ask to see their certificate of completion.

Beyond the basic credential, look for:

- First Aid and CPR certification
- Specialized training in dementia care, palliative care, or mobility support if relevant
- Police record check with vulnerable sector screening

## 3. Ask the right interview questions

Whether you're meeting a caregiver through an agency, a platform, or a personal referral, an in-person conversation matters. Consider asking:

- "Tell me about a challenging shift you've had and how you handled it."
- "How do you adapt your approach when a client is having a difficult day?"
- "What experience do you have with [specific condition — dementia, Parkinson's, post-stroke recovery]?"
- "Are you comfortable with our family checking in regularly?"

Listen as much for tone and empathy as for the content of the answers. Competence and character both matter.

## 4. Start with a trial period

Even when you feel confident about a caregiver, begin with a shorter trial engagement — a few sessions over one or two weeks. This lets both parties assess compatibility without long-term pressure.

During this period, pay attention to how your loved one reacts. Non-verbal cues (relaxed demeanor, willingness to engage, comfort level) often speak louder than anything else.

## 5. Establish clear expectations from day one

Misaligned expectations are the most common source of friction in home care relationships. Before the first shift, establish clear written guidelines covering:

- Arrival and departure times
- Which tasks are in scope (and which are not)
- Communication expectations (daily check-ins, incident reporting)
- Emergency protocols
- Household rules (pets, visitors, phone use)

Clear expectations protect everyone — your family, and the caregiver.
