When a family member needs help at home — whether recovering from surgery, managing a chronic condition, or simply getting older — one of the first questions families ask is: *do we need a nurse or a PSW?*

It's a reasonable question, and the answer matters. Getting the right type of care worker prevents overspending, ensures the right support, and keeps your loved one safe. Here's a clear breakdown.

## What is a PSW?

A Personal Support Worker (PSW) is a trained care professional who assists with **activities of daily living** — the routine tasks that become difficult due to age, illness, or disability. In Ontario, PSWs complete a standardized 6-month college program and are the backbone of home care across the province.

PSWs help with:

- Bathing, grooming, and dressing
- Meal preparation and feeding assistance
- Light housekeeping and laundry
- Companionship and social engagement
- Mobility assistance and fall prevention
- Medication reminders (not administration)
- Accompanying clients to appointments

PSWs are not regulated health professionals in Ontario — meaning they cannot perform clinical or medical procedures. But for most day-to-day care needs, a PSW is exactly the right fit.

## What is an RPN or RN?

Registered Practical Nurses (RPNs) and Registered Nurses (RNs) are regulated health professionals who handle **medical care** — tasks that require clinical training and carry legal accountability.

Nurses handle:

- Wound care and dressing changes
- IV therapy and injections
- Medication administration (not just reminders)
- Catheter and ostomy care
- Health assessments and care planning
- Managing complex medical conditions

Nurses typically cost $50–$90/hr in a private home care context. Their services are partially funded through Ontario's Home and Community Care Support Services (HCCSS) for eligible patients, but demand often exceeds supply.

## When do you need a PSW vs a nurse?

**Choose a PSW when your loved one:**
- Needs help with bathing, dressing, or grooming
- Lives alone and needs companionship and check-ins
- Is recovering from surgery but has no open wounds or IV lines
- Has dementia and needs gentle daily structure
- Requires meal prep and light housekeeping

**Choose a nurse when your loved one:**
- Has wounds that require clinical dressing changes
- Needs injections or IV medication at home
- Has a complex health condition requiring ongoing assessment
- Was recently discharged with specific medical care orders

In practice, many families use both — a nurse visits once or twice a week for clinical tasks, while a PSW provides daily support with living activities.
